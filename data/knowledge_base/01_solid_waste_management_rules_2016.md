# Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016 (MoEFCC, Government of India)
**Source:** Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change Notification, S.O. 1357(E)
**Jurisdiction:** National (India)
**Scope:** Household Solid Waste, Source Segregation, Domestic Hazardous Waste

---

### Rule 3(1)(17): Definition of Domestic Hazardous Waste
"Domestic hazardous waste" means discarded toxic or hazardous items generated at the household level, including:
- Discarded paint cans, pesticide containers, and chemical solvents.
- CFL (Compact Fluorescent Lamp) bulbs, fluorescent tube lights, and mercury-containing devices.
- Discarded or expired medicines (pharmaceuticals), ointments, and syrups.
- Broken mercury thermometers.
- Used needles, syringes, and contaminated sanitary waste.
- Used batteries (button cells, dry cells) generated at households.

---

### Rule 4: Duties of Waste Generators (Households & Citizens)
1. **Source Segregation into Three Separate Streams:**
   Every waste generator, including individual households, residential complexes, and commercial establishments, shall:
   - **(a) Bio-degradable waste (Wet waste):** Segregate kitchen waste, leftover food, fruit/vegetable peels, and garden waste into a separate bin (traditionally Green bin).
   - **(b) Non-biodegradable waste (Dry waste):** Segregate recyclable dry waste such as clean plastics, dry paper, cardboard, glass, metals, packaging containers into a separate bin (traditionally Blue or White bin).
   - **(c) Domestic Hazardous Waste:** Segregate toxic, hazardous, or chemical-containing household items into a distinct separate container, packet, or bin (traditionally Black or Red bag/bin).

2. **Prohibition of Mingling:**
   - No waste generator shall throw, burn, or bury solid waste on streets, open public spaces, water bodies, or vacant plots.
   - Domestic hazardous waste must NEVER be mixed with ordinary wet or dry garbage.

3. **Handover Protocol:**
   - Household generators must hand over segregated domestic hazardous waste separately to the waste collector authorized by the local authority or deposit it at designated municipal collection centers / deposition centers.

---

### Handling Guidelines for Specific Household Items

#### 1. Expired Medicines & Pharmaceuticals
- **Category:** Domestic Hazardous Waste.
- **Handling Action:** Keep separate from daily household garbage. Do NOT flush medicines down the toilet, sink, or storm drain (prevents aquatic contamination and antibiotic resistance).
- **Disposal Pathway:** Place in a sealed bag marked "Domestic Hazardous Waste" and hand over to municipal doorstep collection staff during dedicated collection rounds, or return to authorized pharmacy take-back drop boxes where available.

#### 2. CFL Bulbs & Fluorescent Tube Lights
- **Category:** Domestic Hazardous Waste / Mercury-bearing waste.
- **Handling Action:** Handle with extreme care to prevent glass breakage and toxic mercury vapor release. If intact, wrap carefully in original packaging, newspaper, or bubble wrap.
- **If Broken:** Ventilate the room immediately for at least 15 minutes. Avoid vacuuming. Scoop up debris using cardboard or stiff paper and place in a sealed container or heavy plastic bag.
- **Disposal Pathway:** Hand over separately to authorized municipal collection vehicles or drop off at municipal hazardous waste deposition centers. Do NOT toss into general dry/wet waste bins.

#### 3. Chemical Containers, Paint Cans, & Pesticides
- **Category:** Domestic Hazardous Waste.
- **Handling Action:** Keep original labels intact if possible. Do not rinse into household drains.
- **Disposal Pathway:** Hand over directly to authorized hazardous waste collectors during municipal collection.
